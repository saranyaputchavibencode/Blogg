What happens when AI is on the interviewer's side:

The answer to candidates using AI isn't pretending it doesn't exist — it's using AI to beat their own game. We'll show the clips of when AgentR flags them: on the candidate side, we'll only show them trying to use it; the flagging happens on our side.

[CLIP: hiring-team view showing the flag]

How it catches it: AgentR Guard is a desktop app that runs for the length of the interview and checks the candidate's machine, not their answers. A tool like Cluely is built to hide from a screen share, but it's still a window running on the computer, and that's the level Guard checks. Tab switches and a second monitor get logged too.

What the hiring team gets: one read for the whole session (clean, minor flag, elevated concern or confirmed anomaly) instead of a raw log. A single soft signal like a glance away isn't enough to count against anyone. The candidate doesn't see which checks are running, which is why the candidate clips show only the attempt.

What it doesn't do: a flag isn't a rejection. It marks the session for a person to review, and a person makes the call.

Takeaway / closing line: Preparing with AI is fine, and it always has been. What matters is whether the answer you hear in the interview is coming from the person you're about to hire, and whether you'd know if it wasn't.

FAQs

Is using AI to prepare for an interview cheating?
No. Resume help, company research, mock interviews and practice are all preparation. The line is AI taking part in the interview itself.

Can an interviewer tell when a candidate is using Cluely or Parakeet?
Often not from the video alone, because these tools are built to stay hidden on a screen share and the answers sound natural. [Point to your clips here.]

Does the candidate know they've been flagged?
Candidates are told what's being watched before the interview starts, but they don't see which checks are running or when something gets flagged.

Does a flag mean the candidate is rejected?
No. It pauses the session and marks it for a person to review. Normal nervousness or an unusual setup isn't treated as cheating.

Does this only work on AgentR's AI interviews?
No. Guard can run alongside an interview a person on your team conducts.
